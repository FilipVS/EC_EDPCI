Finland and Poland to co-lead Eastern Flank Watch defence project

Eastern flank leaders expect the European Commission to back the project with funding

Pro
Defence
Charles Cohen Rapporteur

Finland and Poland are committing to co-lead an “Eastern Flank Watch” initiative, together with six other EU countries, leaders from the two countries announced on Tuesday in Helsinki.

Eight EU leaders – representing Finland, Sweden, Estonia, Latvia, Poland, Bulgaria, Romania and Lithuania – met in the Finnish capital for the first-ever Eastern Flank Summit, aimed at discussing the operationalisation of a common project to protect them against potential future Russian attacks. 

“Finland is ready to take the lead with Poland on the Eastern Flank Watch,” Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo told the press after the meeting.  

The eight leaders had already convened in September to define the foundations of a project to counter waves of drone incursions over European skies with Defence Commissioner Andrius Kubilius, together with Hungary and Slovakia.

The Eastern Flank Watch initiative was first pitched by European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen to protect the region’s skies from airspace attacks. It is one of the Commission’s four large-scale defence projects – dubbed ‘flagship’ projects – aimed at getting the continent ready to defend itself by 2030.  

With the EU27 yet to endorse any of the ‘flagship’ projects, the eight countries have moved ahead with their own coalition, which they say is open to other capitals.

The group plans to establish a European Defence Common Project (EDPCI) under the €1.5 billion European Defence Investment Programme (EDIP).

The project would cover ground combat capabilities, drone defence, air and missile defence, border and critical infrastructure protection, military mobility and counter-mobility, as well as strategic enablers, according to a joint statement published shortly before the press conference.  

Some convincing to do 

The eight leaders will now have to present their plan to other capitals and the Commission when they meet at the next EU summit on Thursday.  

“Our next chance is Thursday this week” said Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson.  

Leaders reiterated that they expect the EU executive to back the project it originally proposed with funding. The overall cost of the initiative, however, remains unclear.

To that end, EU leaders will have to apply “political pressure” and prepare a solid pitch to secure support, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said. He pointed to €1.5 billion available under EDIP, recently approved by the Council.  

But the real test will come with the next EU budget, for which the EU executive has earmarked €131 billion for defence and space – 50% for each.  

Advertisement

In October, EU leaders recognised the importance of the eastern flank as a priority. “Finally Europe understands that protection of our eastern border is our common responsibility”, Tusk said on Tuesday.

But they did not formally endorse any of the Commission’s specific initiatives. 

And a similar scenario is now set to unfold later this week. A draft of the upcoming EU summit’s conclusions seen by Euractiv – dated 8 December – still falls short of mentioning the four flagship projects or the EU executive’s Readiness Roadmap pitch.

Not all countries are signed up to Brussels’ eastward pivot. Some heavyweights, including Italy, have previously cautioned that bolstering Europe’s eastern flank should not come at the expense of the south.  

Kubilius floated the idea of a “Mediterranean Flank Watch,” to address these concerns, echoing previous comments that Mediterranean countries were also on the “frontline”, but with “different needs”. 

And the Commission’s list of large-scale defence projects is still subject to change. EU countries are planning a January review to redraw priorities based on their differing national interests, Euractiv previously reported.
