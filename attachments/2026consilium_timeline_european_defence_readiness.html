    • European defence readiness

    Timeline - European defence readiness

    • 2026

      28 September

      Council identifies first five European defence projects of common interest

      The Council approved five European defence projects of common interest to strengthen Europe’s defence readiness and industrial capacity. 

      The projects will bring European countries together to develop critical capabilities in drones and counter-drone systems, maritime and seabed defence, space, air and missile defence, and the protection of Europe’s eastern flank. The projects are now eligible to receive EU funding through the European defence industry programme.

      • European defence industry: Council identifies the first five projects of common interest (press release, 28 September 2026)

    • 2026

      28 September

      Council strengthens EU space threat response architecture

      The Council adopted a decision strengthening the EU space threat response architecture. This is a mechanism designed to respond to increased irresponsible and hostile behaviour in the space domain which has the potential to undermine the safety and security of the EU, its member states and its citizens, and to jeopardise international security and stability.

      In addition, the decision establishes the space security toolbox.

    • 2026

      15 July

      Deal on programme for agile and rapid defence innovation

      The Council and the European Parliament reached provisional political agreement on the proposal to establish the programme for agile and rapid defence innovation (AGILE).

      This new EU instrument is worth €115 million and aims to provide rapid, agile and targeted financial support to small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the defence sector. 

      • Programme for agile and rapid defence innovation: Council and Parliament reach political agreement (press release, 15 July 2026)

    • 2026

      19 June

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders stressed that Europe's defence readiness must be decisively ramped up by 2030 and called for efforts to be stepped up to reduce strategic dependencies and address critical capability gaps.

      They welcomed increased national defence spending and progress in priority capability areas, particularly on drone and counter-drone systems and early-warning, air defence and long-range precision strike capabilities.

      Following repeated violations of member states' airspace, EU leaders stressed the importance of ensuring the defence of all EU land, air and maritime borders, including on the EU’s Eastern flank.

      • European Council, 18-19 June 2026

    • 2026

      17 June

      Military mobility: Council agrees its negotiating position

      The Council approved its mandate for negotiations with the European Parliament on the proposal to establish a framework to enhance EU member states’ ability to move their armed forces seamlessly, at speed and at scale across and beyond the EU.

      The aim is to contribute to Europe’s overall defence readiness and preparedness by introducing an ambitious framework of measures.

      • Military mobility: Council agrees its negotiating position (press release, 17 June 2026)

    • 2026

      15 June

      SAFE: Council concludes agreement with Canada

      The Council adopted a decision formally concluding the agreement between the EU and Canada on the participation of Canadian companies and products originating from Canada in procurement under the Security action for Europe (SAFE) instrument.

      Canada is the first non-European country to participate in the SAFE instrument.

      • SAFE: Council concludes agreement with Canada (press release, 15 June 2026)

    • 2026

      June

      Council activates the national escape clause for Spain

      The Council has activated the national escape clause under the stability and growth pact for Spain. The measure will help Spain transition to higher defence spending at national level without putting its debt sustainability at risk. 

      17 other EU member states have also had their requests to activate the national escape clause approved.

      • Economic governance: Council approves flexibility for Spain to increase defence spending (press release, 12 June 2026)

    • 2026

      10 June

      Council and Parliament strike deal to boost EU defence industry

      The Council presidency and European Parliament negotiators reached a provisional agreement on new legislation that will simplify security and defence procurement, facilitate defence investments and support the defence industry.

      The simplified legislation aims to remove administrative delays in procurement, accelerate permitting-granting procedures, facilitate transfers of defence-related products within the EU and strengthen intra-EU cooperation.

      This agreement will give member states and industry a clearer path to act quickly and will reinforce Europe’s defence capacity.

      • Simplification: Council and Parliament strike deal to boost EU defence industry and readiness (press release, 10 June 2026)

    • 2026

      3 June

      Council agrees programme for agile and rapid defence innovation

      Member states’ representatives approved the Council’s mandate for negotiations with the European Parliament on the proposal to establish a new €115 million programme for agile and rapid defence innovation (AGILE).

      AGILE aims to provide rapid, agile and targeted financial support for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), including start-ups and scale-ups.The new programme is expected to be operational from early 2027.

      • Programme for agile and rapid defence innovation: Council ready to start negotiations with the European Parliament (press release, 3 June 2026)

    • 2026

      10 April

      SAFE: green-light for financial assistance to Czechia and France

      The Council adopted implementing decisions making financial assistance under SAFE available to two more EU member states: Czechia and France. 

      For Czechia, the European Commission has allocated a maximum loan amount of €2.06 billion, while France is set to receive a maximum loan amount of approximately €15.09 billion.

    • 2026

      19 March

      European Council conclusions on defence

      The European Council reaffirmed its determination to decisively ramp up Europe's defence readiness by 2030, reduce strategic dependencies and address critical capability gaps.

      EU leaders welcomed progress on capability coalitions, joint development and procurement as regards drone and counter-drone systems, and initiatives to protect Europe's Eastern flank.

      In light of persistent hybrid campaigns by hostile actors against the EU and its member states, the European Council expects continued efforts to strengthen resilience, enhance preparedness, protect critical infrastructure and prevent, deter and respond to hybrid attacks.

      • European Council, 19 March 2026

    • 2026

      17 February

      Council activates the national escape clause for Austria

      On 17 February 2026 ministers activated the national escape clause for Austria under the stability and growth pact.

      This will facilitate Austria's transition to higher national defence spending while ensuring debt sustainability.

      • Economic governance: Council approves flexibility for Austria to increase defence spending (press release, 17 February 2026)

    • 2026

      11 February

      SAFE: Council green-lights defence funding for eight countries

      The Council adopted a set of implementing decisions making financial assistance under SAFE available to eight EU member states: Belgium, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Denmark, Spain, Croatia, Portugal, and Romania.

      A second batch of Council implementing decisions concerning financial assistance for Estonia, Greece, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Slovakia and Finland has been cleared by EU ambassadors and is expected to be formally adopted by the Council on 17 February.

      In addition, the Council adopted a decision authorising the EU to sign the bilateral agreement between the EU and Canada on the participation of Canadian companies and products originating from Canada in procurement under the SAFE instrument.

    • 2025

      19 December

      Canada to join Security Action for Europe (SAFE)

      The Council endorsed the bilateral agreement with Canada under the Security Action for Europe (SAFE) regulation.

      Canada will be the first non-European country to participate in SAFE, the EU financial instrument providing up to €150 billion to help EU countries boost their defence investments through common procurement.

      • SAFE: member states endorse agreement on the participation of Canada (press release, 19 December 2025)

    • 2025

      18 December

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders strongly condemned all recent hybrid attacks against the EU and its member states.In view of Russia's and Belarus' intensified hybrid campaign, they called for accelerated efforts to strengthen EU resilience and protect critical infrastructure. To deter future attacks, the EU may impose further sanctions on these countries.

      The leaders took stock of progress on ramping up Europe's defence readiness by 2030. In this regard they reviewed the ongoing work at national level, including capability coalitions and priority areas, with a view to launching projects in the first half of 2026.

      • European Council, 18 December 2025

    • 2025

      18 December

      EU adopts measures to boost defence investments

      The Council adopted new measures to support faster, more flexible and better coordinated defence-related investments across the EU.

      The regulation amends five key EU programmes: the Digital Europe Programme, the European Defence Fund, the Connecting Europe Facility, the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform  and Horizon Europe.

      • Council adopts measures to incentivise and simplify defence investments in the EU (press release, 18 December 2025)

    • 2025

      8 December

      Council adopts European defence industry programme

      The Council gave its final greenlight to the European defence industry programme (EDIP), designed to boost the EU's defence readiness.

      Through EDIP, the EU is providing €1.5 billion in the form of grants for the period 2025-2027. The programme is also supporting Ukraine through a dedicated instrument worth €300 million.

      • European Defence Industry Programme: Council gives final approval (press release, 8 December 2025)

    • 2025

      26 November

      Council agrees position on simplification package to boost Europe’s defence industry

      The Council approved its position on a set of proposals intended to simplify security and defence procurement, facilitate defence investments, and improve market conditions for the defence industry.

      The proposals make up the fifth omnibus package under the EU’s simplification agenda.

      • Defence industry: Council agrees position on simplification package to boost Europe’s defence industry and readiness (press release, 26 November 2025)

    • 2025

      5 November

      Agreement to push defence investments under the EU budget

      The Council and the European Parliament negotiators reached a provisional agreement on measures to incentivise defence-related investments in the current EU budget.

      The proposal aims at facilitating faster, more flexible and coordinated investments in the European defence technological and industrial base. In addition, it includes a landmark decision to associate Ukraine to the European defence fund.

      • EU investments in defence: Council and Parliament agree to support faster, more flexible and coordinated investments in European defence (press release, 5 November 2025)

    • 2025

      23 October

      European Council conclusions on defence

      The European Council took stock of work aiming to decisively ramp up Europe’s defence readiness by 2030. It confirmed its determination to deliver at pace and at scale on this objective, so that Europe is better equipped to act and deal autonomously with immediate and future challenges and threats.

      EU leaders condemned the violation of the airspace of several member states and stressed the importance of ensuring the defence of all EU land, air and maritime borders.

      • European Council, 23 October 2025

    • 2025

      16 October

      Agreement on the European defence industry programme

      The presidency of the Council and negotiators from the European Parliament reached a provisional agreement on the European defence industry programme (EDIP), providing €1.5 billion in the form of grants for the period 2025-2027.

      The EDIP aims to boost the EU's defence readiness by enhancing the competitiveness and the responsiveness of the European defence industry.

      • European Defence Industry Programme: Council and Parliament reach provisional agreement (press release, 16 October 2025)

    • 2025

      10 October

      Council approves Germany’s flexibility to increase defence spending

      On 10 October 2025, ministers activated the national escape clause under the stability and growth pact (SGP) for Germany.

      This will help Germany's transition to higher defence spending at national level while ensuring debt sustainability.

      • Economic governance: Council approves Germany’s fiscal expenditure path and its flexibility to increase defence spending (press release, 10 October 2025)

    • 2025

      8 October

      Council agrees to push defence investments under the EU budget

      EU member states agreed on the Council's position on a proposal amending five existing funding programmes: the Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe, the European Defence Fund, the Connecting Europe Facility, and the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform.

      The proposal aims at facilitating faster, more flexible and coordinated investments in the European defence technological and industrial base.

      • Defence: Council agrees position to incentivise defence-related investments in the EU budget (press release, 8 October 2025)

    • 2025

      18 September

      Council authorises negotiations with UK and Canada on their participation in SAFE

      The Council has adopted a decision authorising the opening of negotiations with the UK and with Canada, respectively, on their participation in the €150 billion Security Action for Europe (SAFE) defence loan instrument.

      Negotiations will be conducted by the Commission and will focus in particular on the conditions under which British and Canadian companies and products may be involved in procurements under the SAFE funding instrument.

      • Defence investment: Council authorises negotiations with UK and Canada on their participation in SAFE (press release, 18 September 2025)

    • 2025

      8 July

      Council activates the national escape clause for 15 member states

      On 8 July 2025, the Council activated the national escape clause for 15 member states: Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Greece, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia and Slovenia.

      This will help facilitate their transition to higher defence spending at national level while ensuring debt sustainability.

      • Council activates flexibility in EU fiscal rules for 15 member states to increase defence spending (press release, 8 July 2025)

    • 2025

      26 June

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders called for the acceleration of all aspects of work to ramp up Europe’s defence readiness by 2030.

      They stressed the need to continue to substantially increase expenditure on Europe’s defence and security, and to invest better together, also noting the commitment made at the June 2025 NATO summit by the member states that are also members of NATO.

      • European Council conclusions on European defence and security, 26 June 2025

    • 2025

      23 June

      Council ready to negotiate on the European Defence Industry Programme

      Member state representatives (Coreper) adopted a mandate for negotiations with the European Parliament on the proposal to establish the European Defence Industry Programme and a framework of measures to ensure the timely availability and supply of defence products.

      Through the EDIP, the EU will provide €1.5 billion in the form of grants by 31 December 2027.

      • European Defence Industry Programme: Council ready to start negotiations with the European Parliament (press release, 23 June 2025)

    • 2025

      27 May

      EU adopts Security Action for Europe (SAFE)

      The Council adopted a regulation setting up the Security Action for Europe (SAFE) instrument. SAFE will provide loans worth up to €150 billion to help member states make rapid and significant increases in their defence investments. The allocation of the budget will be demand-driven.

      SAFE opens up a new chapter in the cooperation with non-EU countries and is the first pillar of the European Commission's ReArm Europe Plan/Readiness 2030.

      • SAFE: Council adopts €150 billion boost for joint procurement on European security and defence (press release, 27 May 2025)

    • 2025

      20 March

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders called for an acceleration of all work strands to ramp up Europe’s defence readiness within the next five years.

      They also called for the implementation of the actions identified in the European Council conclusions of 6 March 2025 in the field of capabilities to be initiated as a matter of urgency and for continued work on the relevant financing options.

      • European Council, 20 March 2025

    • 2025

      6 March

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders stressed that Europe must become more sovereign, more responsible for its own defence and better equipped to act and deal autonomously with immediate and future challenges and threats with a 360° approach. 

      In this context, the European Union will accelerate the mobilisation of the necessary instruments and financing in order to bolster the security of the European Union and the protection of our citizens. 

      • Special European Council, 6 March 2025

    • 2024

      27 June

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders discussed urgent, immediate and medium-term defence needs and European defence initiatives. They also held a first discussion on options for mobilising funding for European defence.

      They invited the Council, member states, the Commission and the High Representative to take work forward on all strands, in line with their respective competences, in particular on critical capability gaps, the European Defence Industry Strategy and the European Defence Industry Programme, with a view to its adoption by mid-2025.

      • European Council, 27 June 2024

    • 2024

      21 March

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders discussed about increasing the EU's overall defence readiness and capabilities to match its needs and ambition, with a strengthened technological and industrial base.

      The leaders stressed the importance of accelerating implementation of the Strategic Compass and that a stronger and more capable EU in the field of security and defence will contribute positively to global and transatlantic security and is complementary to NATO.

      • European Council, 21-22 March 2024

    • 2023

      14 December

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders underlined that more needs to be done to strengthen the EU’s operational readiness and enhance its defence investment and capability development.

      In this regard, they called for existing measures to be urgently implemented to facilitate joint procurement and to increase the European defence industry’s production capacity and for a European defence industrial strategy to strengthen Europe’s defence technological and industrial base.

      • European Council, 14-15 December 2023

    • 2023

      9 October

      Adoption of the European Defence Industry Reinforcement through Common Procurement Act (EDIRPA)

      The Council adopted a regulation establishing an instrument for the reinforcement of the European defence industry through common procurement (EDIRPA), paving the way for its entry into force.

      The regulation will incentivise cooperation in defence procurement between member states in order to increase solidarity, prevent crowding-out effects, increase the effectiveness of public spending and reduce excessive fragmentation in defence procurement.

      • EDIRPA: Council greenlights the new rules to boost common procurement in the EU defence industry (press release, 9 October 2023)

    • 2023

      7 July

      Deal on the Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP)

      The Council presidency reached a provisional agreement with the European Parliament representatives on the Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP).

      The agreed regulation will urgently mobilise €500 million from the EU budget (in current prices) to support the ramp-up of the manufacturing capacities for the production of ground-to-ground and artillery ammunition, as well as missiles.

      • ASAP: Council and European Parliament strike a deal on boosting the production of ammunition and missiles in the EU (press release, 7 July 2023)

    • 2023

      29 June

      European Council conclusions on defence

      EU leaders underlined the need to strengthen the technological and industrial base of the European defence sector.

      They welcomed the agreement on the European defence industry reinforcement through the common procurement act and called for swift action on the delivery and joint procurement of ammunition and on a proposal for a European defence investment programme.

      • European Council, 29-30 June 2023

    • 2023

      28 June

      Deal on the European Defence Industry Reinforcement through Common Procurement Act (EDIRPA)

      The Council reached a provisional agreement with the European Parliament on the European defence industry reinforcement through common procurement act (EDIRPA).

      The regulation will incentivise EU member states to jointly procure weapons, thereby ensuring interoperability, economies of scale and - ultimately - a strong European defence industry.

      • EU defence industry: Council and European Parliament agree on new rules to boost common procurement (press release, 27 June 2023)

    • 2023

      23 June

      Council ready to negotiate on the Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP)

      The Council agreed a negotiating mandate on the proposed regulation establishing the Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP).

      By introducing targeted measures, the proposed ASAP regulation aims to address the current shortage of ammunition and missiles, as well as their components, thereby implementing track 3 of the plan agreed by the Council in March 2023 to urgently deliver ammunition and missiles to Ukraine and help member states refill their stocks.

      • Boosting ammunition production in the EU defence industry: Council ready to negotiate with the European Parliament (press release, 23 June 2023)

    • 2023

      20 March

      Agreement on the three-track proposal to provide Ukraine with artillery ammunition

      In a joint session gathering EU foreign affairs and defence ministers, the Council agreed on the three-track proposal to urgently provide Ukraine with artillery ammunition, either coming from existing stocks or jointly procured.

      • Foreign Affairs Council, 20 March 2023

    • 2022

      15 December

      European Council conclusions on defence

      During the December European Council, EU leaders call for the swift adoption of the proposed regulation establishing the European Defence Industry Reinforcement through common procurement act (EDIRPA).

      They also recommend an intensification of efforts to facilitate and coordinate joint procurement, including replenishing stocks in light of support to Ukraine.

      • European Council, 15 December 2022

    • 2022

      1 December

      General approach on the European Defence Industry Reinforcement through Common Procurement Act (EDIRPA)

      The Council agreed on a general approach on the proposed regulation establishing the European Defence Industry Reinforcement through common procurement act (EDIRPA). 

      In its general approach, the Council confirmed the Commission proposal to commit €500 million from the EU budget for the period from the entry into force of the regulation to 2024.

      • EU defence industry: Council reaches general approach on boosting common procurement (press release, 1 December 2022)

    • 2022

      10-11 March

      Versailles declaration: EU leaders agree to spend more and better on defence

      In the face of the new security environment created by the Russian invasion of Ukraine, member states have announced their intention to spend more and better on defence in order to better protect their citizens.

      EU heads of state or government, meeting in Versailles on 11 March, committed to ‘bolster European defence capabilities’ in light of the Russian military aggression against Ukraine.

      • Versailles declaration, 10-11 March 2022

    • 2020

      14 December

      Provisional agreement reached on setting-up the European Defence Fund

      The Council reached a provisional political agreement with European Parliament’s representatives on a regulation establishing the European Defence Fund, in the context of the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) for 2021-2027.

      The new European Defence Fund will support collaborative actions and cross-border cooperation throughout the EU, at each stage of the industrial cycle relating to defence products and technologies.

      The programme design will ensure participation of defence industries of all sizes, including SMEs and mid-caps, across the EU,  thus strengthening and improving defence supply and value chains.

      • Provisional agreement reached on setting-up the European Defence Fund (press release, 14 December 2020)

    • 2019

      19 November

      Council adopts its position on the European Defence Fund


      The Council adopted its position (partial general approach) on the European Defence Fund proposed by the European Commission in the context of the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) for 2021-2027.

      The European Defence Fund aims to foster innovation and allow economies of scale in defence research and in the industrial development phase by supporting collaborative projects.

      • European Defence Fund: Council adopts its position (press release, 19 November 2019)
