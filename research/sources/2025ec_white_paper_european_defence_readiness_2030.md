## Raw summary (EDPCI-relevant)
- Proposes that Member States "urgently pool their efforts" on critical capability gaps, "including through a set of **Defence Projects of Common European Interest**, **to be defined by Member States** and that would benefit from **EU incentives**". (Note the early name: "Common *European* Interest" — later "Common Interest".)
- Seven priority capability areas: air and missile defence; artillery systems; ammunition and missiles; drones and counter-drone systems (unmanned aerial, ground, surface and underwater vehicles with advanced software and sensors); military mobility; AI, quantum, cyber and **electronic warfare** (incl. protecting/denying use of the electromagnetic spectrum); strategic enablers and critical infrastructure protection (incl. surveillance, maritime domain awareness, **protection of space and secure communications assets**).
- Conditions named for successful capability projects: clear alignment with European Council guidance, stable long-term commitment, agreed governance per capability type, EU funding and incentives to help Member States mobilise budgets.

## Key conclusions
- ✅ **Origin of the EDPCI concept in the 2025 policy sequence:** the White Paper says the projects are **defined by Member States**, with EU incentives — consistent with the final design where states submit proposals and only states receive EDPCI funding (Reg. Art. 35).
- ✅ The capability list shows where RF/electronics sit **in the policy framing** (electromagnetic-spectrum protection and denial; secure communications; sensors on unmanned systems) — a ⚠️ pointer for relevance only; the EDPCI texts themselves still name no RF or battery items.
